Draft Den is where you write the manuscript, and also where you keep everyone in it, every place they go, and every promise the story makes. It runs on your own computer, needs no account, and never needs the internet.

Chapter OneThe page

You open the app and there is the page. Bold, italics, headings, quotes, lists: the usual, out of your way. It saves itself half a second after you stop typing, and every chapter is a plain Markdown file in a folder you own. If Draft Den vanished tomorrow, your book would still open in any text editor.

plain text, always. no lock-in

A chapter breaks into scenes. A scene owns its paragraphs, so it moves, counts its own words and disappears with them, like scenes in a real draft. Select a sentence and pin a private note to it, or a numbered footnote that ships with the book.

scenes, notes and footnotes

#

When you need the room to go quiet, focus mode leaves only the page. When you wander too far, chapter versions let you walk the prose back to where it was yesterday. Spelling is checked in several languages and grammar in English, all on your machine, without a single word leaving it.

focus mode, versions, offline spellcheck

Chapter TwoThe people in it

By chapter twelve you have forty names and a bad memory. Draft Den reads your prose as you write and quietly underlines the people and places it knows. Hover one:

it knows her nicknames too

Open a chapter and a short note says who is in it, when each of them was last seen and what has changed since. And when you step back, a grid shows the whole cast across the whole book:

the "Previously" strip

Chapter ThreeKeeping promises

Every book makes promises. A gun on the wall in chapter two owes the reader a shot. Mark the line where you plant it, mark the line where it pays off, and Draft Den keeps the ledger. Open promises wait on your home screen, oldest first.

setups and payoffs

It also notices when the story contradicts itself. A character you killed in chapter nine walks into chapter fourteen. Someone is in two cities on the same morning. The prose calls a girl twenty when her birthday says seventeen.

continuity checks, no AI

Chapter FourThe world

Your world gets its own shelves. Characters, places and cultures have pages with portraits, galleries, relationships and facts that change over time: an eye lost in a battle stays lost in every later chapter, and not a page before.

state over time

Describe your continents, kingdoms and cities, and Draft Den paints the map from them, coasts, rivers, roads and borders, in the style of an old atlas. Build your own calendar with its months, weekdays and eras, and the timeline puts every event on it.

painted maps, your own calendar
Writer / Draft Den / 6

Chapter FiveYours alone

Draft Den lives on your computer. There is no account to make, nothing to subscribe to, and no one reading over your shoulder: no tracking, no cloud, no AI trained on your pages. Signed backups let you carry the whole vault anywhere.

offline, private, backed up

When the book is done, it becomes an EPUB or a print-ready PDF with your cover on the first page. For your editor, translator or cover artist there is a series bible: one page about your world as of any chapter, with nothing from later on.

EPUB, PDF, spoiler-safe bible

The app speaks English, French, Spanish, Italian and Arabic, and writes left to right or right to left.
